---

## Day One question

Does this feel like **today's ritual** or **today's task**?

**Today's task.**

Lived Mission is: domain eyebrow → title → summary → numbered Soft Plate checklist → **Mark complete**. The parent does not practice with the child inside the screen; they honor a checkbox. Success then opens a decision stack (Coach · Back · Ask Amy). That is task completion + upsell fork — not a parenting ritual.

If Mission had been designed by Apple + Headspace on Day One, “Mark complete” would never be the ritual. The ritual would be the practice itself — held, short, human — and completion would be a quiet breath, not a form submit.

## Psychology (first)

| Current psychology | Nest Presence psychology |
|--------------------|--------------------------|
| Prove you finished | Be present for one small moment |
| Checklist compliance | Guided togetherness |
| Mark done → unlock next product | Finish → rest in care |
| Success = trophy check + funnel | Success = held · optional next whisper |
| Domain “Speech” as filing label | Concern lived in the step, not stamped as taxonomy |
| Empty play surface (read then tick) | The screen hosts the practice, not the audit |

**Core translation:** Mission must stop being a self-report form. Completion may still exist as product state (frozen) — but it cannot be the *felt* center of the experience.

## What Mission would feel like if AmyNest had never been a SaaS product

You enter a quiet room with one clear invitation — the same soft step Amy named on Today. There is no “Speech” stamp. There is no numbered audit card that looks like homework. The guidance is simply how to be with your child for a few minutes: sit near, try one sound, wait.

When the moment is over, you are not asked to *mark* anything like a chore app. You are offered a gentle close — the sense that you showed up. A calm line says it was a real step. Confidence without points. Then the door back to Today is obvious. If Amy Coach can help later, she waits at the edge of the room — she does not rush the hug with a product pitch.

Mission is not a task you clear. It is a short ritual you keep.
