# Founder Translation Review — Landing (Nest path only)

**Surface under review:** AmyNest V2 Landing after Production Translation  
**Scope:** This screen only. Not Signup. Not Front Door. Not the app shell.  
**Mode:** Four independent design juries. No redesign. No protection of the existing work.  
**Question each team must answer:** Would we ship this?

---

## Verdict board

| Team | Ship? |
|------|:-----:|
| Apple Design | **NO** |
| Headspace Design | **NO** |
| Airbnb Design | **NO** |
| Linear Design | **NO** |

**Unanimous: do not ship.**

The first breath is better than the purple AI landing it replaced. That is not the bar. The bar is whether a world-class design team would put this on a home screen and stand behind it. None would.

---

## 1. Apple Design Team

### Would we ship this?

**NO**

### Why?

Apple ships *inevitable* composition — one idea, one action, silence around it. This screen performs Nest Presence for a few hundred pixels, then reverts to a long marketing warehouse with Soft Plate wallpaper. The hero section still contains a second, quieter product pitch (badges, age line, secondary CTA, store badges, QR, “AI co-pilot”). That is not Law of Three. That is Law of Three with the old page stuffed in a drawer that still opens inside the fold on many devices.

“Try on Web” is operator language, not human language. The wordmark still reads as an AI product. The page never earns the quiet it claims.

### Every objection

1. First viewport is not sealed. Receded cluster (badges → stores → QR → AI co-pilot) lives in the same hero column; tall phones still see a second product layer before scroll.
2. Dual `data-v2-law="hero"` / dual `support` markers — composition roles are confused in markup and in the eye.
3. Primary CTA copy (“Try on Web”) is platform logistics, not an emotional invitation.
4. Nav still offers Sign in + Get the app beside a page whose primary job is a third action — three exits before the parent has arrived.
5. Brand string “AmyNest AI” (and SEO title with AI) fights Nest Presence identity.
6. 3D mascot + float animation risks uncanny “AI character demo,” not held human craft.
7. Soft Plate repeated on every below-fold module = one material, zero hierarchy — looks manufactured, not considered.
8. Infinite length: age band, marquee, command center, spotlights, infant section, AI modes, stages, mid install, tech, problems, how-it-works, trust, testimonials, final CTA, download, footer — Apple would cut this to a fraction or reject the page as a brochure.
9. Store badges + QR in the hero stack after the primary CTA reintroduce install anxiety into a “calm arrival.”
10. “Free to start · Child-safe · Privacy-first” is a trust slogan strip — competent, not distinctive; reads App Store compliance, not craft.
11. Below-fold Bloom CTAs multiply; Bloom stops being inevitable.
12. Age chips with emoji remain toy/product UI under a calm hero — tonal whiplash.
13. Research marquee is credential theater; Apple does not marquee its way to trust.
14. Screenshot grids (“What parents actually open”) are catalogue energy — Hardware Marketing would reject as feature dump.
15. Testimonials with gradient “result” pills still smell growth-hack even after Soft Plate restyling.
16. Opacity whisper/recede is still a demotion trick, not spatial editing — Apple would ask why the content is still there.
17. Page is a theme fork (`nest ? … : legacy`) — the Nest path is a costume on a marketing skeleton, not a designed object.
18. No photographic human truth — only avatar + product screenshots. Apple’s emotional landings show the life, not the widget.
19. Motion (float + staggered fade) is polite but generic; nothing feels inevitable or proprietary.
20. Would fail Design Review at “scroll past the button” — the page breaks its own promise within one thumb flick.

## 2. Headspace Design Team

### Would we ship this?

**NO**

### Why?

Headspace sells a *feeling before a feature*. The Nest hero almost gets there — quieter type, softer field, one primary. Then the page becomes a parenting SaaS tour that reactivates anxiety: age selectors, science citations scrolling by, problem grids (tantrums, screens, sleep), AI modes, patent lines, install walls. That is the opposite of a sanctuary entry. A sleep-deprived parent who felt a breath of calm is immediately asked to shop categories of their stress.

We would not ship a meditation home that opens into a feature marketplace.

### Every objection

1. Emotional arc collapses after the CTA — calm → catalogue → credential → conversion. Betrayal of the first feeling.
2. “AI co-pilot” copy (even whispered) reintroduces tool anxiety Mid-arrival.
3. Problem grid (tantrums, screen time, sleep…) names every fear without holding the parent — Headspace never opens with a menu of pain.
4. Science citation marquee is academic noise; trust is felt, not ticker-taped.
5. “Command Center” language is the wrong nervous system for a calm product.
6. Age personalization as emoji radio pills is gamified onboarding chrome on a landing meant to soothe.
7. Multiple install surfaces (hero stores, mid install, final, footer download) create conversion pressure loops — anti-mindfulness.
8. Testimonials framed as outcome badges (“Clearer evenings…”) are performance parenting, not compassion.
9. Soft Plate sameness below fold feels institutional, not warm.
10. Float on the character is restless; stillness would be more Headspace than bobbing mascot.
11. “Personalized daily plans for sleep, meals, learning, and emotions” is a feature list inside the support line — still product copy, not care copy.
12. Infant / learning / sound / health spotlights are a content mall — cognitive load after a quiet hello.
13. Patent / tech / routine engine sections read as startup diligence, not care.
14. Page never gives permission to *stop* — no quiet ending, only more CTAs.
15. Dual identity: Nest breath on top, growth landing underneath — we would call this unfinished wellness UX.
16. Would not put this in front of a user in crisis mode at 2 AM.

## 3. Airbnb Design Team

### Would we ship this?

**NO**

### Why?

Airbnb’s bar for an entry surface is *belonging through place and people*. This Landing sells a companion and a catalogue. There is no host, no home, no lived parenting moment — only a floating character and product screenshots in Soft Plate frames. Hospitality design would ask: where is the family? Where is the room at night? Where is the specific, local truth?

“Try on Web” is a cold door. We open doors with places and stories, not platform verbs.

### Every objection

1. Hero image is a mascot, not a place or a person — no belonging signal.
2. No photography of real parents/children in a Nest-quality emotional register (screenshots ≠ hospitality imagery).
3. Copy is category claim (“One parenting companion”) not a specific invitation into a life.
4. Primary CTA is channel logistics (“Try on Web”) — Airbnb would never lead with “Try on Web.”
5. Below fold is a product matrix (hubs, stages, modes) — marketplace of features, not a host story.
6. Soft Plate cards as the dominant pattern feel like a template design system demo, not a brand world.
7. Spotlights with tags (“Speech,” “Study,” “Vaccines”) are inventory labels.
8. Stage grid (Newborn → School) is a segmented catalogue, not a journey told with warmth.
9. Trust section as icon chips (“No ads for kids”) is policy, not hospitality.
10. QR / store badge density treats the parent as an install funnel, not a guest.
11. Age gate early in the scroll is a form before a welcome.
12. Brand mark whispered in caption size under-sells identity without replacing it with a stronger place metaphor — neither bold brand nor rich place.
13. “Meet AMY” energy survives as AI co-pilot caption — still a product introducer, not a host.
14. Final sections repeat the same ask (install / try) without escalating emotional specificity.
15. The Nest lighting wash is a skin; the structure is still a 2020s startup landing page.
16. Would not ship as a brand homepage — might pass as a temporary feature page only after extreme cut.

## 4. Linear Design Team

### Would we ship this?

**NO**

### Why?

Linear ships *ruthless clarity and craft density*. This translation improved tokens and first-breath hierarchy, then refused to delete. Keeping every section and “receding” it is not craft — it is compliance with a content freeze dressed as design. The Soft Plate grid is repetitive. The hero still carries a junk drawer. Motion and materials are consistent enough to look intentional, but the information architecture is still a marketing novel.

We would ship a short, sharp entry. We would not ship this length with this many equal modules.

### Every objection

1. Content freeze (“keep all sections”) made true craft impossible — translation without deletion is half a job.
2. Receded hero junk drawer (badges, secondary CTA, stores, QR, AI line) proves hierarchy was applied with opacity, not editing.
3. Soft Plate on ~every module → visual fatigue; no elevated rarity, no Atmosphere breathing room between chapters.
4. Too many Bloom instances below fold — primary action inflation.
5. Eyebrow + H2 + body + card grid repeats the same section template ~10 times — pattern becomes noise.
6. Marquee animation is decorative motion without product job.
7. Icon wells in Soft Plate for AI modes / tech / problems are interchangeable — no distinctive craft object.
8. “Try on Web” CTA label fails craft test: unclear outcome, weak verb, platform-centric.
9. Nav whisper + page whisper + caption whisper → under-contrast risk; quiet ≠ illegible hierarchy.
10. Dual codepath (`nest ? A : B`) is a maintenance smell; craft systems don’t costume legacy pages forever.
11. Age emoji controls are optically loud against Constitution type — unresolved component language.
12. Spotlight image+tag+link cards are generic product marketing components, not Linear-grade density.
13. Trust / patent / science blocks are status packing — Linear would cut to one sharp proof or none.
14. Footer link farm is fine; the problem is everything above it competing at similar weight.
15. No single memorable craft detail that says “only AmyNest” — orb emit is close, then drowned.
16. Performance/attention cost of a mega-landing fights the calm brand claim.
17. Ship gate: first fold alone is *almost*; full page is a hard no until cut or radically paced.
