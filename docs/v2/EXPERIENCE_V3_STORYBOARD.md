# Moment 1 — Opening AmyNest

### Emotion
Quiet relief.  
Not excitement. Not overwhelm.  
The feeling of walking into a calm room after a loud day.

### Purpose
Make the parent feel:  
**“I am in the right place.”**

### Transition
From phone chaos → into AmyNest stillness.  
The world narrows. Noise drops. Attention softens.

### Visual feeling
Soft light. Wide breath. One clear center of gravity.  
Nothing competing. Nothing selling. Nothing crowded.  
Premium emptiness with one living presence: Amy.

### Motion feeling
Slow inhale.  
Gentle rise.  
No bounce. No sparkle storm. No feature parade.  
Like a curtain opening onto one quiet stage.

### Sound feeling (optional)
A soft ambient bed. Almost silence.  
One warm tone that says: arrive.

### Trust signal
Nothing asks yet.  
Nothing demands account, plan, or payment.  
The product proves composure before it asks for commitment.

### Conversion goal
First Open completed with emotional safety.  
Desire to continue — not obligation.

### Conversion goal

# Experience Laws of Feeling (Not Governance)

These are redesign constraints expressed as feeling only:

1. **Calm before ask** — never demand before value.  
2. **One child presence** — never generic parenting fog.  
3. **One next thing** — never a mall of modules.  
4. **Dignity in success** — never carnival rewards.  
5. **Memory as care** — never guilt.  
6. **Signup as keep** — never gate.  
7. **Premium as continuity** — never pressure.

---

# Redesign Test

Before any future redesign is accepted, it must pass this storyboard test:

Can a first-time parent move through these seven moments and honestly say:

> “AmyNest helps me know my child’s next right thing today —  
> and I want to keep that.”

If not, the redesign is incomplete.
