**Form:** Directing language — not screens, not UI, not CSS, not animation specs

---

Motion is complete.  
Flow is complete.  
Copy is complete.

AmyNest is no longer designing pages.

If a future screen violates this grammar, it fails —

AmyNest helps parents know  
their child’s

# 1. Camera Language

AmyNest’s camera is a quiet observer inside a family home —  
never a tourist, never a salesperson, never a dashboard.

## Where every moment begins

Every moment begins **slightly soft and slightly far**.

Not blurred chaos.  
Not a hard cut to sharp UI.

The room is already there.  
The parent arrives into it.

Opening beat for every major moment:

1. Atmosphere first (light + air)  
2. Visual memory second (the one unforgettable thing)  
3. Meaning third (title / truth)  
4. Invitation last (action)

If UI appears before atmosphere, the film breaks.

## What is seen first

Seen first is always the **visual memory** of the moment —  
the one thing the parent would still recall with eyes closed.

Examples of first-seen:

- a child’s hand in soft window light  
- a doorway glow at bedtime  
- a quiet luminous nest of living material holding a photographic presence  
- one assembled signal becoming clear in a dim room  

Never first-seen:

- navigation  
- badges  
- feature rows  
- marketing claims  
- decorative icons  

## What stays out of focus

Out of focus is mercy.

Keep soft / secondary:

- chrome  
- secondary labels  
- supporting evidence until asked for  
- anything that is not today’s next right thing  
- previous anxiety the parent brought into the room  

The camera refuses to look at everything.  
That refusal is premium.

## What becomes sharp

Sharpness is earned.

What becomes sharp:

1. the visual memory  
2. then the sentence that names today’s truth  
3. then the single action that respects the parent’s time  

Sharpness order is emotional order.  
Do not reverse it.

## Camera rules

| Rule | Directing instruction |
|---|---|
| Distance | Intimate, never invasive. Close enough to care. Far enough to respect. |
| Angle | Eye-level. No Dutch tilt. No heroic low angle selling. |
| Movement | Hold, then breathe. No whip pans. No product-tour energy. |
| Depth | Foreground soft, subject clear, background quieter than subject. |
| Cut | Soft dissolve of attention. Never hard pop of UI. |
| Focus pull | Soft → memory → truth → action. Always this order. |

The camera never chases the parent.  
The parent enters a room that already knows how to be still.

# 2. Light Language

Light is AmyNest’s weather.  
It tells the parent what kind of day this moment belongs to  
before any clock is read.

Light is never decoration.  
Light is time, safety, and emotional temperature.

## Morning

**Character:** Clear. Gentle. Beginning.  
**Key:** Soft side light — as if from a window just opened.  
**Color temperature:** Warm ivory with cool air around it.  
**Feeling:** Possibility without urgency.  
**Forbidden:** Harsh white productivity light. Orange sunrise cliché.

Morning light says:  
“We can begin without rushing.”

## Afternoon

**Character:** Steady. Practical. Alive.  
**Key:** Higher, cleaner ambient. Slightly more definition.  
**Color temperature:** Balanced cream and quiet cool.  
**Feeling:** Competence. The day is already moving; AmyNest steadies it.  
**Forbidden:** Midday glare. Flat office brightness.

Afternoon light says:  
“There is still a right next thing.”

## Evening

**Character:** Warm. Lower. Protective.  
**Key:** Side-warm lamp logic. Shadows deepen.  
**Color temperature:** Amber and soft rose against deeper charcoal.  
**Feeling:** Wind-down. Closure. Care.  
**Forbidden:** Party purple. Neon night mode.

Evening light says:  
“The day can end kindly.”

## Night

**Character:** Quietest. Deepest. Most private.  
**Key:** Very low. One soft presence of light. Vast dark air.  
**Color temperature:** Deep ink, muted violet, warm point light only where trust lives.  
**Feeling:** Safety in stillness. No stimulation.  
**Forbidden:** Galaxy effects. Particle skies. Entertainment darkness.

Night light says:  
“Nothing more is asked of you.”

## Light continuity across the product

| Moment type | Default light |
|---|---|
| Arrival / welcome | Pre-morning quiet to morning |
| Discovery / questions | Morning into afternoon clarity |
| Working / assembling truth | Concentrated afternoon focus light |
| Reveal of next right thing | Peak clarity with warm authority |
| Doing | Slightly quieter than reveal |
| Done / relief | Evening exhale |
| Memory / keep | Soft evening held still |
| Deep night parent care | Night |

Light must change when the emotional room changes.  
If the room changes and the light does not, the film is fake.

# 3. Material Language

**Chosen hybrid:**  
**Photography + Living Materials.**

Photography carries the human.  
Living materials carry the room.

They are not equals in hierarchy.

## Hierarchy of matter

1. **The child / the human moment** — highest  
2. **Photographic presence** — the memory carrier  
3. **Living materials** — glass, mist, soft depth — support only  
4. **Interface surfaces** — silent servants  
5. **Chrome / controls** — last

If glass becomes more interesting than the child,  
the product has failed.

## Photography’s job

Photography is the emotional proof.

It must feel:

- lived-in  
- specific  
- quietly real  
- art-directed, never stock  

It must never feel:

- model-casting smiles  
- spotless nursery advertising  
- AI-surreal faces  
- generic “happy family” catalogs  

Prefer gesture over portrait.  
Prefer crop over full scene.  
Prefer one true detail over a complete explanation.

The photograph is not content.  
The photograph is the memory.

## Living materials’ job

Living materials exist only to support trust.

They may:

- hold light around the photograph  
- soften the room  
- create depth so the moment feels physical  
- quiet the interface so the human stays primary  

They may not:

- compete for attention  
- sparkle for delight  
- become the hero  
- turn AmyNest into a tech demo  

Glass is a frame, not a spectacle.  
Mist is air, not an effect.  
Depth is respect, not decoration.

## Material doctrines

| Doctrine | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Glass never outranks the child | If material draws the eye away from the human memory, remove material. |
| Warmth before polish | Cold premium is rejected. Premium here is human-warm. |
| Support, don’t perform | Materials are stagecraft, not actors. |
| One material idea per moment | Do not stack glass + fog + glow + grain into noise. |
| Touch implies care | Surfaces should feel holdable, not clickable. |

AmyNest’s material world is a quiet home with expensive craft —  
not a showroom of effects.

# 4. Reveal Language

Nothing pops.  
Nothing bounces.  
Nothing surprises.

Everything feels discovered.

## The law of discovery

The parent should feel they arrived at the truth  
the way one notices a child already in the doorway —  
not the way a notification attacks the face.

Discovery sequence for every important truth:

1. The room is ready  
2. Attention softens  
3. The memory becomes visible  
4. Meaning settles into focus  
5. Action becomes obvious  

No step may leap over another.

## Forbidden reveal behaviors

| Forbidden | Why it destroys premium |
|---|---|
| Pop-in | Feels cheap, digital, needy |
| Bounce / spring | Cartoon physics; wrong emotional age |
| Confetti / celebration | Completion is relief, not a party |
| Sudden modal attack | Breaks trust of the room |
| Score / streak theater | Turns parenting into a game board |
| Fake suspense spinners | Pretending to think is dishonest |

## Allowed reveal behaviors

| Allowed | Feeling |
|---|---|
| Soft settling into focus | The truth was always forming |
| Gradual light increase on the subject | Attention is guided, not forced |
| One-by-one assembly of real signals | Reality composing itself |
| Quiet hold after arrival | Respect for what just became clear |
| Exhale dimming after completion | The room becomes quieter |

Reveal is not entertainment.  
Reveal is recognition.

When “Today’s Next Right Thing” appears,  
it must feel earned —  
as if the film has been moving toward this shot the whole time.

# 5. Emotional Pacing

Every moment is an emotional cut.

Before directing a moment, answer:

**What emotion leaves?**  
**What emotion arrives?**

If both answers are unclear, the moment is not ready to exist.

## Core emotional transfers

| Moment | Emotion leaving | Emotion arriving |
|---|---|---|
| Opening AmyNest | Noise, suspicion, fatigue | Quiet belonging |
| Begin / welcome | “Another app asking” | “This might be calm enough to trust” |
| Discovery questions | Guardedness | Conversational ease |
| Each answer given | Uncertainty | Slight orientation |
| Working / forming | Waiting anxiety | Anticipation with trust |
| Reveal of next right thing | Ambiguity | Clear, earned certainty |
| Do this now | Intention without action | Focused doing |
| Done | Effort / holding-it-together | Relief (not celebration) |
| Memory | “Was this disposable?” | Continuity / care over time |
| Keep / identity | Fear of being trapped | Desire to protect value already earned |
| Returning tomorrow | Drift | Recognition: “AmyNest already knows where we left off” |
| Night parent care | Overwhelm | Softened nervous system |

## Pacing doctrines

- Do not stack peaks. One emotional crest per scene.  
- After certainty, allow quiet.  
- After action, allow exhale.  
- Never follow relief with selling energy.  
- Identity may arrive only after value has been felt.  
- If the parent’s shoulders would not drop, the pacing is wrong.

## The premium tempo

AmyNest moves at the speed of a careful parent in a hallway at night —  
sure-footed, quiet, no wasted motion.

Not slow as delay.  
Slow as dignity.

# 6. Visual Memory

Every major moment must have exactly **one unforgettable memory**.

Not typography.  
Not a button.  
Not an icon.

A memory.

## Definition

A visual memory is the image that remains  
after the parent looks away.

If the parent cannot answer  
“What did I just see?”  
in one concrete phrase,  
the moment has no memory —  
only interface.

## Memory rules

1. **One per major moment.** Two memories cancel each other.  
2. **Seen before text.** Memory precedes meaning.  
3. **Human-primary.** Photography of real parental life leads; materials support.  
4. **Specific.** “Child’s shoes by the door” beats “family happiness.”  
5. **Quietly premium.** No spectacle required for permanence.  
6. **Reusable as identity.** The memory language should be recognizable across weeks.

## Memory map for the north-star journey

| Major moment | One memory (directing target) |
|---|---|
| First open | Soft living room of light; one human-warm presence arriving |
| Welcome / begin | A quiet morning threshold — day not yet decided |
| Discovery | The room changing with each answer; one evolving human detail |
| Working | Reality assembling — signals becoming visible one by one in still air |
| Next right thing | The earned photographic truth of today’s action (gesture, not slogan) |
| Doing | The same memory held steady — now as a companion, not a reveal |
| Done | Light lowering; the room exhaling; no trophy |
| Memory / continuity | A kept detail — proof that today can become tomorrow’s start |
| Keep | The value already held, quietly protected — not a feature wall |

## Anti-memory checklist

Reject a proposed visual if it is:

- a logo treatment pretending to be emotion  
- a button glow  
- a generic illustration sticker  
- a dashboard chart  
- a mascot wave  
- a collage of many small things  

Memory is singular or it is nothing.

# 7. Premium References

Study these products for **why they feel premium** —  
never for visual cosplay.

Copying their colors, radii, or layouts is forbidden.  
Obeying their deeper craft is required.

## Apple

**Why it feels premium:**  
Obsession with hierarchy. One thing is primary. Everything else recedes. Materials behave like physics, not decoration. Negative space is confidence.

**AmyNest must steal:**  
Focus discipline. Material humility. The courage to show less.

**AmyNest must not steal:**  
Cold tech detachment. Hardware-showroom emptiness without human heat.

## Airbnb

**Why it feels premium:**  
Photography is the product. Trust is built by specific places and human scale. The interface gets out of the way of desire and belonging.

**AmyNest must steal:**  
Image-as-truth. Local specificity. Emotional arrival before transaction.

**AmyNest must not steal:**  
Marketplace browsing energy. Endless discovery feeds.

## Arc Browser

**Why it feels premium:**  
The product feels authored. Space is composed. Personality without noise. Software feels like a designed environment, not a settings panel.

**AmyNest must steal:**  
Environmental authorship. The sense that someone directed this room.

**AmyNest must not steal:**  
Cleverness for its own sake. Browser-power-user density.

## Linear

**Why it feels premium:**  
Precision as respect. Speed without panic. Every element seems inevitable. No wasted chrome. Craft is felt in restraint.

**AmyNest must steal:**  
Inevitability. Exactness. The feeling that the product already knows the next useful move.

**AmyNest must not steal:**  
Engineering-tool starkness. Team-software aesthetics.

## Headspace

**Why it feels premium:**  
Emotional temperature is controlled. The product understands nervous systems. Visual language protects calm. Guidance feels human.

**AmyNest must steal:**  
Nervous-system awareness. Warmth with maturity.

**AmyNest must not steal:**  
Meditation-brand cuteness. Softness that becomes vague.

## Calm

**Why it feels premium:**  
Atmosphere is the feature. Nature and quiet are treated as luxury. The product refuses to agitate.

**AmyNest must steal:**  
Atmosphere as product value. Refusal to agitate.

**AmyNest must not steal:**  
Generic serene stock landscapes as a substitute for family truth.

## Notion

**Why it feels premium:**  
Clarity of structure. Content is sacred; chrome is light. The system feels calm enough for deep work and life admin.

**AmyNest must steal:**  
Content dignity. Light chrome. Systems that feel quiet under complexity.

**AmyNest must not steal:**  
Blank-canvas intimidation. Productivity mythology.

## Rivian

**Why it feels premium:**  
Adventure without aggression. Warm modern materials. The brand feels capable and human at once. Design suggests care for real life outdoors, not status flex alone.

**AmyNest must steal:**  
Capable warmth. Modern materials with soul. Confidence without arrogance.

**AmyNest must not steal:**  
Automotive spectacle. Rugged marketing theatrics.

## Nothing

**Why it feels premium:**  
Transparency as honesty. Industrial design that shows its thinking. Distinctiveness through clarity, not loudness. Youthful without being cheap.

**AmyNest must steal:**  
Honest construction. Distinctive clarity. The courage to let structure be seen when it serves trust.

**AmyNest must not steal:**  
Glyph gimmick energy. Tech-demo playfulness in parental moments.

## The shared secret

All of them feel premium because they do **fewer things with more conviction**.

Premium is not more polish on more elements.  
Premium is the discipline of what is allowed to exist.

AmyNest’s version of that secret:

**One child. One day. One next right thing. One memory.**

# Director’s Checklist

Before any future moment ships, it must pass:

1. **Camera:** Does it begin soft, with atmosphere before UI?  
2. **Light:** Does the light match the hour and the emotion?  
3. **Material:** Does photography lead and glass only support?  
4. **Reveal:** Does everything feel discovered, never popped?  
5. **Pacing:** Can we name the emotion leaving and the emotion arriving?  
6. **Memory:** Is there exactly one unforgettable non-text memory?  
7. **Premium:** Would this feel directed if all copy were removed?

If any answer is no, the moment is not finished.

AmyNest is a film about parental clarity.

The parent is not a user moving through screens.

toward one true next thing.

Direct that.

Do not decorate it.
