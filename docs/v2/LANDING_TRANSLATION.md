**Mode:** Translation only. No code. No mockups. No redesign exploration.  
**Frozen:** Constitution · Product · Features · Brain · Architecture · Routes  
**Question:** If AmyNest had been designed from Day One as Nest Presence — would this Landing exist?

**Answer:** Almost none of it. What exists today is a SaaS brochure wearing Nest clothing. A Nest Presence Landing would be nearly empty.

**Delete rule:** For every visible element — would Apple keep it? Headspace? Airbnb? Linear?  
If **all four answer NO → DELETE.** No softening. No restyle.

---

## Five-second trust test

What absolutely must remain for a tired parent to trust Amy within five seconds?

| Must remain | Job in five seconds |
|-------------|---------------------|
| Amy’s presence | “Someone is here with me.” |
| One calm promise | “This is for my parenting — not another tool.” |
| One way in | “I know what to do next.” (existing route unchanged) |
| One quiet trust line | “Safe enough to begin.” |
| Brand whisper + Sign in | Orient returning parents without selling |

Everything else fails the five-second test. Delete it.

## Would this Landing exist from Day One Nest Presence?

**No — not as it stands.**

A Nest Presence AmyNest would never have built a product catalogue as the front door. It would have built a threshold: presence, promise, one step in, quiet trust. The current Landing exists because AmyNest was grown as a SaaS marketing site. Nest Presence does not inherit that structure. It deletes it.

Routes stay. The *brochure* does not.

## What would the Landing look like if AmyNest had never been a SaaS product?

Do not draw it. Describe it.

You open the door and the room is almost empty.

There is soft Nest light — morning, evening, or night — the same world as the product, not a campaign gradient. Amy is simply present, still, not performing. Below her, a few calm words: that she is a parenting companion, and that she grows with your child. Not a list of what she does. Not sleep, meals, learning, emotions as a feature stack. Just the relationship.

Under that, one warm action — the same route you already have into Front Door — worded like an invitation to begin, not like a platform channel. Under the action, one quiet line that says it is safe to start. Nothing else competes.

In the corner, Sign in for parents who already belong here — small, mist, easy to miss if you are new. At the very bottom, if you scroll at all, only the quiet legal breaths: Privacy, Terms, Support. No app store wall. No QR. No science ticker. No screenshots of hubs. No stages. No proof theatre.

You do not learn the product on this page. You arrive. Five seconds is enough to feel held. Then you step through.

That is the Landing Nest Presence would have built on Day One — a threshold, not a website.
