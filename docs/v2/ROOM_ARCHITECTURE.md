# AmyNest Room Architecture — The Home

**Mode:** Architecture of feeling only. No UI. No layout. No routes. No implementation. No redesign.  
**Law:** AmyNest is not an application. AmyNest is a **Home**. Every destination is a **Room**.  
**Identity:** Nest Presence  

---

## The Home

AmyNest is one dwelling under one sky.

The parent does not open apps. They cross thresholds.  
Rooms share the same air, the same hour of light, the same hush.  
Nothing in the Home shouts a different brand of weather.

A room is defined by what it holds — and by how much it refuses to hold.

## The Threshold

*(was Landing)*

### Atmosphere
Outdoor hush meeting indoor warmth — the porch of the Home. Air is cool enough to clear the head, warm enough to invite. No marketplace noise. No fog of other brands.

### Light
One soft key from above the door. Enough to see the face of the house. Not a showroom. Not neon. Evening Nest hour preferred; never a nightclub.

### Silence
Almost total. The Threshold is the quietest public room. If more than a few sounds compete, it has failed.

### Dominant object
**Amy’s presence** — still, near — and one calm promise that this is a parenting companion that grows with the child.

### Supporting object
One warm invitation to enter. One quiet trust breath (safe to begin).

### Attention
Rests on Amy, then inevitably on the door in.

### Emotional purpose
“I’ve arrived somewhere calm.” Not “I found a tool.”

### Leave with
Willingness. Soft curiosity. Shoulders slightly lower.

### Transition from previous
From the open world (store, link, search). Weather of the street falls away at the porch.

### Transition into next
Into the Vestibule (welcome) or, for those who already belong, toward the Living Room. Never into a neon alley.

## The Vestibule

*(was Front Door — named here because the Home needs a welcome hall)*

### Atmosphere
Intimate entry hall. Smaller than the Living Room. Personal without interrogation. The Home learns your name and what keeps you up — gently.

### Light
Close, warm, low. A held candle sense — not a corridor of forms.

### Silence
High. Only Amy’s breathing presence and the next soft question.

### Dominant object
**The breath / Amy’s held presence** — the first ritual of belonging.

### Supporting object
One step of knowing (age, name, worry) — never a stack of equal doors.

### Attention
On the feeling of being received.

### Emotional purpose
You are not a lead. You are a guest becoming known.

### Leave with
Recognition. “She already sees something of us.”

### Transition from previous
From Threshold — same air, closer walls.

### Transition into next
Into the Living Room — the day begins without a costume change.

## The Keep

*(was Signup)*

### Atmosphere
A quiet side chamber off the hall — not a vault of chrome, not a nightclub gate. The Keep exists to protect what the Home already started. Air same as the Living Room. Hour unchanged.

### Light
Identical Nest hour to the room you came from. No purple storm. No rotating spectacle.

### Silence
High. Only the necessity of keeping. No slogans. No “smart parenting” echo.

### Dominant object
**The reason you are here** — continuity: the child’s name, the worry, the progress worth protecting.

### Supporting object
One warm act of keeping (create / continue). Social keys exist as quiet spare keys on a hook — never the chandelier.

### Attention
On “save this place with Amy,” not on Meet-AMY theatre.

### Emotional purpose
Relief that the journey can be held safely — not onboarding anxiety.

### Leave with
Belonging secured. Ready to return to the Living Room or continue the path you came from.

### Transition from previous
From Living Room, Practice close, Study, Hearing Room, or Continuity — always as a soft lock, never as the climax of care.

### Transition into next
Back into the room that asked for keeping — same air, trust unbroken.

## The Living Room

*(was Today)*

### Atmosphere
The heart of the Home. Morning or Nest-hour calm. One clear place to sit with the day. Not a control panel. Not a wall of doors.

### Light
Session hour — morning lift, evening dusk, or night hush — same world as every other room. Soft key over the day’s step.

### Silence
Medium-high. Space enough that one sentence from Amy can be heard. If four invitations speak at once, the room has become an office.

### Dominant object
**Today’s one step** — the living ask for this child, this concern.

### Supporting object
**Amy’s short breath** — she remembers; she is with you.  
(Focus and greeting are one thought, not two competing furnishings.)

### Attention
Naturally rests on the step, then on the single warm action to begin it.

### Emotional purpose
Companionship for the day. “I know what matters today.”

### Leave with
Orientation and readiness — not decision fatigue.

### Transition from previous
From Vestibule or Threshold — arrival into daily life. From Practice Room — return home after the ritual. From Keep — unlocked and held.

### Transition into next
Into the Practice Room (the step). Help may call from the Hearing Room only when sought. The Study and Continuity Room are not furniture in the Living Room — they are other chambers, entered when earned.

## The Practice Room

*(was Mission · Success is the room’s last light, not a separate product)*

### Atmosphere
A small room for togetherness — like a quiet corner where parent and child meet for a few minutes. Not a classroom. Not a chore clipboard.

### Light
Gentle, focused — a pool of light on the practice itself. No trophy glare at the end.

### Silence
Very high during practice. Guidance is soft speech in the air, not a numbered audit. At the close: deeper hush — relief.

### Dominant object
**The living practice** — the human moment (sound, pause, nearness). The room hosts the ritual; it does not host a checkbox.

### Supporting object
Quiet guidance (how to be with the child). At close: one honor sentence — “that was a real step.”

### Attention
On the child and the moment — not on “marking complete.”

### Emotional purpose
Presence → **relief**. Not achievement. Not unlock.

### Leave with
Lighter. “I showed up.” Confidence without points.

### Transition from previous
From Living Room — one door, one purpose.

### Transition into next
Back to Living Room — the natural close.  
The Study may be mentioned like a note on the table — never a second spotlight in the doorway. Continuity and Hearing Rooms stay out of the afterglow.

## The Study

*(was Coach)*

### Atmosphere
A long-view room — still Nest air, slightly more composed. Like sitting with someone who has listened for a while. Not a setup lab. Not a progress factory.

### Light
Steady, warm, unhurried. No spinner weather. No purple foreign sky.

### Silence
High. Being seen requires quiet. If the room fills with stages, checklists, and “generating,” it has become a workshop.

### Dominant object
**Amy’s recognition** — she already knows the worry and the child; she offers to walk the longer path.

### Supporting object
One consent to continue when ready. One soft way back to the Living Room.

### Attention
On feeling understood — not on building a plan object.

### Emotional purpose
“I am seen for the long road.” Dignity. No onboarding rush.

### Leave with
Willingness to be guided — or peaceful decline (not right now).

### Transition from previous
From Practice afterglow (earned whisper) or from Living Room when the longer path is sought — never as a twin sofa beside today’s step.

### Transition into next
Into real guidance when trust is whole.  
The Keep may be needed to protect the journey — as a side chamber, never as the Study’s applause. Never fake waiting as the Study’s furniture.

## The Hearing Room

*(was Ask Amy)*

### Atmosphere
A close, soft room for speaking what is hard right now. Intimate. Immediate. Not a gallery of clever questions. Not a public AI hall.

### Light
Warm and near — as if Amy leaned in. Same Nest hour. No chatbot glow.

### Silence
Very high until the parent speaks. Then Amy’s voice — not a wall of prompts speaking first.

### Dominant object
**The help for this worry** — bedtime, talking, this morning — named, known.

### Supporting object
Amy’s knowing (“I already hold today’s focus”). Then the conversation as continuation of care — not a drop into another building.

### Attention
On being heard. One invitation to begin — not three starter plaques.

### Emotional purpose
Held help. Vulnerability met with answer, not with a gate.

### Leave with
A bit of clarity or comfort. Shoulders down. Not “I hit a paywall.”

### Transition from previous
From Living Room or Practice when help is needed — sought, not stacked as furniture in the Living Room.

### Transition into next
Stay until held; then back to Living Room.  
The Keep, if needed to save the thread, waits *after* the first help — never as the doorman.

## The Continuity Room

*(was Premium)*

### Atmosphere
A private letter-room. Still Nest. No shop floor. No rate-card glare. The air says stay, not buy.

### Light
Soft and even — hospitality lamp, not retail spots on price tags.

### Silence
High. One conversation: will you keep walking with Amy?

### Dominant object
**The invitation to continue care** — for this child, this worry.

### Supporting object
The quiet how (the means to continue) — present, misted, never the chandelier. Restore is a spare key in the drawer.

### Attention
On staying with Amy — not on comparing plans.

### Emotional purpose
Continuity. Choice without shopping shame. Cancel without exile (“you’re still with Amy”).

### Leave with
Either calmly continued — or calmly free to return later. Never “I browsed a catalog.”

### Transition from previous
From Living Room or Keep when continuity matters — not as a chapter sofa in the Living Room; not as the Practice Room’s exit tax.

### Transition into next
Back to Living Room — care resumes. Never into a louder store.

## The Child’s Room

*(was For Child)*

### Atmosphere
A small world that belongs to the child — play-light, soft wonder — only if something living is here.  
If the room is empty, **the door is not in the hallway.** Nest does not hang a child’s name on a vacant room.

### Light
Gentler, slightly brighter play of Nest light — still the same sky. Alive objects cast soft presence. Empty air with only hope is not light; it is vacancy.

### Silence
Warm silence with one living sound or object — not the silence of a warehouse.

### Dominant object
**One real next thing for the child** — a breath of play, learn, or care that exists.

### Supporting object
Hope around that living thing — “Amy will build with you” as light, not as the only furniture.

### Attention
On the child’s next moment — not on saving an empty space.

### Emotional purpose
Aliveness. Belonging for the child. Joy or calm play — never loneliness labeled hope.

### Leave with
A sense that the child’s world has begun — or, if the door was closed, no false promise.

### Transition from previous
From Living Room when the child’s world is sought — and only when the room is furnished with life.

### Transition into next
Stay in play/learn/care, or return to Living Room.  
The Keep protects a real space — never a plaque.

## Laws of the Home (architecture)

1. **One sky** — every room shares Nest hour and air.  
2. **One voice** — Amy’s; never a different product dialect per room.  
3. **One dominant object** per room — Law of Three as architecture.  
4. **Silence is a material** — if a room is loud, it is wrong.  
5. **Care before Keep** — the Keep never stands in the doorway of Hearing or Practice afterglow.  
6. **No furniture from other houses** — neon, prompt galleries, rate cards, empty named doors.  
7. **Transitions keep the weather** — leaving a room never means changing climate.  
8. **Empty rooms do not get names on the door.**

## How the parent walks the Home

```
Street → Threshold → Vestibule → Living Room
                ↘ Practice Room → (relief) → Living Room
                ↘ Hearing Room → Living Room
                ↘ Study → (when earned) → …
                ↘ Continuity Room → Living Room
                ↘ Child’s Room (only if alive)
                ↘ Keep (when something must be held) → return
```

Always one Home. Always Nest Presence.
